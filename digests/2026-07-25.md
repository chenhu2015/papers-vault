TRACE (0.91) closes gap #8 in the most principled way yet. Prior credit assignment papers: GRPO (trajectory-level uniform), TACO/EGRSD (heuristic high-credit tokens), OPPO (Bayesian token↔trajectory recursion), MURPHY (multi-turn retrospective credit for code), BDA (turn-level counterfactual). TRACE provides the turn-level bridge via the simplest possible state value: the model's implicit belief about eventual success, read directly from the frozen reference model's log-probability. This requires no additional networks, no process labels, and no value training — it is entirely derived from the trajectory structure itself. The +28.4 point gain on Qwen3-4B on BrowseComp-Plus is among the largest single-paper gains in the vault, establishing that dense turn-level credit (not just sparse trajectory reward) is load-bearing for long-horizon multi-tool-call search. The SFT-free property (pure RL from scratch) and open-web transfer are additional strengths: TRACE achieves what most prior agentic RL requires SFT cold-start to even begin learning.

Dark Room (0.85) is the most structurally important new finding. It explains WHY dense rewards fail under GRPO — not just empirically, but via a formal mechanism: GRPO's std normalization makes the z-scored advantage invariant to the shaping coefficient in all-fail groups, so any bounded dense reward becomes unbounded gradient pressure regardless of scale. The variance-profile criterion generalizes this: a dense signal is structurally safe for GRPO z-scoring only if its within-group variance decays as the policy masters the behavior. Signals that do not decay (e.g., prediction of the next observation, which always has remaining entropy) will amplify pathologically. The auxiliary-loss channel finding is immediately actionable: SEED (also today) implements exactly this — it converts completed trajectories into auxiliary loss signals rather than reward signals. The Dark Room paper retroactively explains why SEED's design is correct (and why naive reward-channel injection of the same signal would fail). This also connects to TRACE: TRACE's log-ratio state values are computed offline from the frozen reference model and used to shape the per-step advantage before GRPO normalization — they are never injected into GRPO's reward channel and therefore escape the all-fail group amplification pathology.

PATR (0.85) establishes the process-scorer-guided branch of the tree-rollout design space. BPO (Jul 22) uses policy entropy (oracle-free structural signal); PATR uses a process scorer (learned or prompted PRM). PATR's SWE-Bench results (+5.0) are important: BPO was evaluated on code agents but not on SWE-Bench's repository-level complexity. Together, BPO + PATR define the two primary strategies for adaptive branching in multi-turn agentic RL: structural uncertainty (BPO) and process quality (PATR). The compatibility with standard GRPO/RLOO means either can be applied to existing training pipelines without algorithmic changes.

PATS (0.84) partially closes gap #15 from the training-context angle. OpenForgeRL (Jul 24) found that harness choice affects RL trainability; PATS shows that training-time context adaptation — independently of harness architecture — can compensate for weak policy exploration. The evidence-card mechanism is orthogonal to OpenForgeRL's harness-proxy (which records environment interactions) and SEED's distillation (which converts trajectories into loss signals). PATS operates at the prompt level: it tells the policy what to try next based on what the current policy has already tried. The zero-deployment-cost property (scaffold discarded at inference time) makes PATS compatible with any existing harness.

PROVE (0.80) closes the training-data quality gap in MCP-server RL alongside ToolVerse (Jul 24, environment scale). The state-machine synthesis pipeline is a direct solution to the silent-failure problem: if training queries reference entities that don't exist in the current server state, tool calls fail silently and the training signal is noise. By grounding queries in live-sampled server state, PROVE ensures that every generated trajectory is executable. The adaptive efficiency penalty is the reward-level solution to verbosity in tool-use RL — complementary to APO's STCR (length penalty on negative MLLM responses, Jul 24) but specifically targeting the verbosity-via-recall-credit failure mode in multi-step tool orchestration.

**New thread — GRPO structural pathologies:** Dark Room + Clip-Low/Clip-High (Jul 24) + RL-ZVP (zero-variance starvation, earlier) form a cluster of papers characterizing structural failure modes in GRPO normalization specifically. Dark Room adds a third mode: all-fail group amplification of bounded dense rewards. The variance-profile criterion provides a unified test for whether a dense signal is safe to use with GRPO's z-scoring. This cluster suggests that GRPO's std normalization is the primary source of instability in LLM RL training, distinct from the reward signal design itself.
