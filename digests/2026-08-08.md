**EnvACE extends the agentic RL training paradigm to a fourth structural class: world rehearsal.** The Aug 7 taxonomy had three structural classes for handling the environment interaction bottleneck: AXPO (prefix-fixing resampling of all-wrong subgroups), RAPO (retrieval-augmented trajectory injection), DPEPO (explicit diversity reward across simultaneous environments). EnvACE introduces a fourth: eliminating the external environment entirely by having the policy play both actor and environment roles. The key structural distinction is that AXPO/RAPO/DPEPO all require real or synthesized environments during training; EnvACE's policy IS the environment. The world model is not a separate component — it is internalized into the same parameter set as the policy, jointly optimized with task-success rewards. At test time, this enables private pre-execution rehearsal (the policy can simulate consequences before committing to a tool call) — a capability that none of the other three classes provides.

**S2L-PO introduces model-size hierarchy as a third mechanism for diversity provision in GRPO.** The vault's rollout diversity taxonomy now has three mechanisms: (A) explicit reward shaping — DPEPO's Diverse Action Reward and Diverse State Transition Reward; (B) retrieved trajectory injection — RAPO's retrieval-augmented step-level exploration; (C) model-size-induced policy diversity — S2L-PO's small-model natural explorer. The key insight is that smaller models have higher pass@k relative to sample count not because of token-level randomness but because their policy-level diversity is temporally correlated and logically coherent. This makes small model rollouts a cleaner exploration signal than temperature-injected noise. The progressive annealing strategy (offline small-model rollouts → large model's own sampling) is the same dual-phase structure as RAPO's exploration-to-exploitation transition.

**MCPO provides a contrastive counterpart to the multi-task RL decoupling thread.** The vault's multi-task RL approaches have focused on separation (Switch-Reasoner's dual-level mode balance, Parallel-RL's task decoupling). MCPO flips this: instead of separating domains to prevent interference, it explicitly identifies which cross-domain trajectories are transferable and uses them as positives in a contrastive objective. The result is that cross-domain signal becomes beneficial transfer rather than harmful interference — without needing explicit domain separation. MCPO also aligns intra-domain correct rollouts, which is structurally analogous to how DPEPO's diversity rewards push rollouts apart (MCPO pulls same-domain correct rollouts together while DPEPO pushes same-environment rollouts apart).

**EnvFactory and EnvACE represent polar approaches to the agentic RL environment bottleneck.** EnvFactory scales environment construction through full automation (autonomous discovery, verification, trajectory synthesis from real-world APIs). EnvACE eliminates environment construction by internalizing dynamics into the policy. These are complementary: EnvFactory is the right approach when real environment fidelity is required (e.g., for tool-use agents that must interact with actual APIs at deployment time); EnvACE is the right approach when the goal is policy-internalized world knowledge that transfers without environment scaffolding. The topology-aware trajectory synthesis in EnvFactory addresses the "over-specified trajectory" problem — synthetic trajectories that resemble instruction sequences rather than natural queries reduce RL training effectiveness.

**SciDisco's DiscoPO introduces verification-grounded turn-level credit, orthogonal to divergence-based calibration.** The vault's turn-level credit taxonomy has focused on distributional/divergence-based approaches (ADRS-TVA, PCSD, OCSD, DASH, RP-OPSD). DiscoPO introduces a fundamentally different axis: outcome-grounded credit based on verifiable analytical evidence production. A turn that produces a verifiable analytical claim receives positive credit; a turn that does not, does not — regardless of divergence from a teacher. This is closer to process reward models (PRM) than to the OPD calibration literature, but applied at the turn level in a scientific discovery context. Gap between divergence-based and verification-grounded turn-level credit remains open.
