**ATOD resolves the temporal scheduling question the OPD cluster left open.** All prior vault OPD papers (BCSD, NOPD, OGLS-SD, GC-OPD, Distill-What-Student-Can-See, etc.) focus on what to distill and how to weight it per token/step. None ask when during training to apply distillation vs. RL. ATOD's annealing answer — heavy distillation early for convergence speed, heavy RL late for reward ceiling — is the first vault paper to treat the training schedule itself as the primary design variable. The T-DUR component (per-turn disagreement gating) is separately interesting: it is a soft version of what RTPO (Aug 20) does with turn reversal, applied at the distillation-signal level rather than the trajectory level.

**SAT introduces a new training paradigm not represented elsewhere in the vault.** Every prior vault training paper trains a single model (or a single model + a lightweight auxiliary head). SAT trains a team of independent models jointly but without a coordinator — the block-coordinate structure means each agent update is a standard single-model RL training step, but the advantage estimator conditions on the other agents' current policies. The plug-and-play invariance theorem has a direct practical implication: once a team is trained with SAT, individual agents can be swapped for stronger models as they become available, without retraining. This is a fundamentally different scaling behavior than single-model RL: capability can be improved by substitution, not just by continued training.

**PACT bridges the EDGE experience-internalization thread and the SFT-vs-RL-conflict thread.** EDGE (Aug 27) uses self-generated experience at training time; PACT uses expert traces. Both keep inference prompt-only. The key difference: PACT's trace-conditioned RL surrogate allows the RL objective to be evaluated in an expert-trace context that was never available at rollout — a form of counterfactual advantage that does not require environmental counterfactual rollouts. This is structurally analogous to GEAR's evidence-grounding reward (Aug 23) evaluating the model's visual evidence claims against annotations, but PACT applies the idea to trajectory-level advantage estimation rather than per-step reward.

**ZeroTIR's scaling law is the most concrete empirical result about tool-use emergence under RL.** The vault's prior tool-use papers (EvolveVLA Aug 20, MAPO Aug 26, EDGE Aug 22) all assume some tool-use capability exists and study how to improve it. ZeroTIR is the first vault paper to study the emergence of tool use from scratch and find a quantifiable monotone relationship with training compute. The implication is that tool-use capability is not a qualitative threshold requiring initialization but a continuously learnable behavior whose frequency scales with RL compute. This shifts the framing from "how to initialize agents with tool use" to "how much RL compute to invest to reach a given tool-use frequency."
