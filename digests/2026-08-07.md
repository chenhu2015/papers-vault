**DASH introduces the 4th orthogonal calibration axis for OPSD teacher supervision.** The vault's teacher calibration taxonomy now has four axes: (A) ADRS-TVA — confidence-return correlation: does teacher confidence predict rollout success within the group? (B) PCSD — temporal persistence: does the teacher maintain a consistent advantage over consecutive positions? (C) OCSD — scaffold isolation: does the teacher's score reflect observation content vs. replay scaffold structure? (D) DASH — sequential divergence history: does the local divergence fit the temporal discrepancy sequence observed so far in this rollout? DASH's key insight is that the same local divergence magnitude can carry different information depending on whether it follows a consistently-high or consistently-low discrepancy sequence — a 0.3 divergence early in a high-discrepancy rollout signals something different than the same magnitude in a low-discrepancy rollout. The four axes operate at different temporal granularities: A at the group-outcome level, B at the token-sequence level, C at the replay-mechanism level, D at the within-rollout discrepancy-sequence level. Gap #19 expands to A×B×C×D.

**RP-OPSD adds a token-type axis orthogonal to all four divergence-based axes.** Where DASH/PCSD/ADRS/OCSD all adapt supervision based on divergence quantities (magnitude, history, correlation, scaffold difference), RP-OPSD adapts by functional token type: reasoning-control and state-update tokens (pivots) vs. surface realization tokens. This axis is detection-based rather than magnitude-based — it identifies *which* token positions carry reasoning direction, not *how much* divergence they exhibit. RP-OPSD is structurally analogous to FutureBridge-OPD at the state level: FTB selects high-disagreement states via future trajectory simulation, RP-OPSD selects reasoning-pivot tokens via reference-conditioned distributional shift. The combined state-selection (FTB) × token-type-selection (RP-OPSD) × token-divergence-calibration (DASH/PCSD/ADRS/OCSD) × scaffold-isolation (OCSD) framework would be the most complete multi-level teacher supervision architecture described in the vault.

**DPEPO extends the agentic RL exploration taxonomy to a third structural class.** AXPO (Aug 3) addressed the credit side of tool-use failures via prefix-fixing resampling of all-wrong subgroups. RAPO addressed the exploration side via retrieval-augmented step-level trajectory injection. DPEPO introduces a third class: explicit diversity reward shaping via simultaneous multi-environment interaction. The key structural difference: AXPO and RAPO both modify the rollout sampling strategy to increase diversity; DPEPO makes diversity a first-class optimization objective via the Diverse Action Reward and Diverse State Transition Reward. This is structurally closer to how PRISM (Aug 3) treats multi-objective alignment: rather than hoping diversity emerges from sampling, it becomes an explicit training signal.

**MMPO provides a unified theoretical lens for the Gap #16 all-fail filtering literature.** The vault has characterized 10+ approaches to all-fail group handling (CIGPO, TAO-RL, AXPO, LSPO, Dark Room, etc.). MMPO's moment-transformation framework now provides the unifying formal account: standard GRPO optimizes only the first moment (mean failure probability); all-fail group filtering interventions implicitly reduce the variance (2nd moment) and skewness (3rd moment) of the failure-probability distribution by preventing the degenerate all-fail state. Dark Room (Aug 6) explained the mechanism (z-score cancels dense shaping in all-fail groups); MMPO explains the goal (reducing higher moments that GRPO leaves uncontrolled). Together, they provide the complete formal account of why Gap #16 interventions work.

**Probing RL vs SFT closes the representational half of Gap #21.** Gap #21 (skill lifecycle vs. vanilla RL compositional emergence threshold) has two sub-questions: (a) what representational changes does RL induce that enable compositional discovery, and (b) at what task complexity threshold do these RL-induced representations become insufficient without explicit skill management? This paper answers (a): RL restructures representations toward more hierarchical (deeper-layer-critical) and linearly separable (correctness-predictable) structures. The Aug 6 RL Post-Training paper answered the behavioral (a): compositionality emerges behaviorally. Question (b) — the complexity threshold — remains open. Gap #21 narrows to: given these representational and behavioral characterizations, at what task complexity does the linear separability / hierarchical depth of RL representations fail to support compositional generalization without external skill scaffolding?
