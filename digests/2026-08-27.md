**The experience-internalization vs. skill-library axis is now a coherent design dimension in the vault.** The Aug 26 skill-evolution cluster (SkillForge, Skill-R1, Skill1, SKILL0) all maintain explicit skill representations — either an append-based library, a separate generator, or a frequency-decomposed policy. EDGE and Evolving-RL (today) both target the same "benefit from past experience without inference-time retrieval" goal but via implicit internalization into base policy parameters. The two approaches vary on: (a) representation explicitness (library vs. parameters), (b) internalization mechanism (distillation vs. co-evolutionary supervision), (c) whether the extractor is explicitly trained (Evolving-RL: yes; EDGE: no separate extractor). Neither EDGE nor Evolving-RL cite any Aug 26 skill papers, again suggesting independent development of converging ideas.

**SRPO is the first vault paper where the model generates its own credit signal through self-reflection.** All prior vault credit-assignment papers require an external signal source: SPA-RL needs a trained progress estimator, MileGPO uses template-based milestones, DataPRM has an environment-aware verifier, GEAR has annotated key evidence, IAPO reads the influence graph from the rollout structure. SRPO's self-reflection patches are generated by the same model being trained — which raises a faithfulness question: do the reflection patches accurately identify which steps were causal vs. which steps the model rationalizes post-hoc as errors? The Why2Speak paper (outside cap) directly addresses this faithfulness problem in a different setting; the intersection with SRPO is unstudied.

**IAPO adds a fourth credit-assignment approach to the vault taxonomy.** The causal graph in IAPO (completed rollout → typed influence-dependency graph → advantage routing weights) is closer to "Credit Without Ground Truth"'s ideal (Aug 21) than SPA-RL's progress estimator: it operates on causal structure from the rollout rather than correlation learned from reward. However, IAPO's graph is still correlation-based at the structural level — it identifies that action A influenced outcome B via tool response C, but does not establish that removing A would have changed B (counterfactual criterion). The MileGPO PCC vs. IAPO influence graph vs. counterfactual ground truth comparison remains an open empirical gap.

**SPO++ and Missing Old Logits (outside cap) together constitute a systematic correctness audit of asynchronous agentic RL.** SPO++ fixes advantage normalization in SPO; Missing Old Logits fixes importance ratio semantics in async PPO. Both identify that decoupling rollout generation from policy optimization introduces subtle correctness failures in the training objective, not just efficiency tradeoffs. These two papers are the only vault entries that treat async RL infrastructure as the primary object of study rather than a training setting for application papers.
