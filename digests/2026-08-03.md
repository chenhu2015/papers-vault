**AXPO introduces a new structural class for Gap #16 in the multimodal domain.** All nine prior Gap #16 approaches operate within text-only GRPO. AXPO diagnoses two interacting failure modes unique to multimodal tool-use GRPO that do not arise in single-modality math reasoning: (1) low tool-attempt rate (~30% of rollouts), meaning tool-use experience is scarce regardless of group composition; and (2) all-wrong tool-using subgroups (~40% of questions), meaning that even when tool use is attempted, all rollouts in the group fail, zeroing the gradient. AXPO's prefix-fixing resampling is a new mechanism class for Gap #16: it does not modify the reward (CIGPO, RDPO), filter the group (TAO-RL, TACO/OGAR), inject proxy rewards (ProGPO), use contrastive reflection (GRSD), or scaffold with external completions (LSPO) — it restructures *which* completion tokens are resampled by holding the thinking prefix fixed. LSPO (also today) introduces a parallel new class: sampling-time LoRA scaffolding with IS-corrected splicing of successful completions from the adapter back into the GRPO batch.

**SPyCE closes a gap in the vault's skill lifecycle synthesis for the multimodal domain.** The skill lifecycle synthesis (SkillRise + Bayesian-Agent + ReSkill + UCOB) was built from text-only agentic tasks. SPyCE extends this into multimodal RL with two specific contributions: (1) a hierarchy that separates visual execution skills from workflow-level tool orchestration priors, reflecting the structural asymmetry between local visual operations and global task planning that does not exist in text-only agents; (2) online co-evolution within a single RL training run rather than across task sequences (SkillRise) or offline retrieval (Bayesian-Agent, ReSkill). Together with AXPO and IAPO, SPyCE completes a multimodal RL triangle: AXPO addresses the sample-efficiency problem (how to recover gradient from tool-use failures), SPyCE addresses the knowledge-reuse problem (how to retain and transfer successful tool patterns), and IAPO addresses the reward-signal problem (how to reward correct tool attention in the absence of outcome labels).

**PRISM opens a new axis: policy-space decomposition for multi-reward LLM RL.** All prior vault papers either optimize a single reward or scalarize multiple rewards into one. PRISM's decomposition into reward-specific positive policies + a shared negative policy is a third approach: it avoids gradient conflict not by separating training stages (as in SFT→RL curricula) or by re-weighting reward signals (as in GOPO's ordinal transform), but by factoring the policy space itself. The inference-time composition mechanism makes PRISM's approach uniquely controllable: the user can adjust per-dimension weighting without retraining. The combination of PRISM's policy decomposition with GOPO's ordinal reward normalization per sub-policy would be a natural Gap #18 extension.

**IAPO provides the second paper (after PRPO) to use visual attention as a VLM RL training signal.** PRPO's RVD (Gap #7) rewards spatial faithfulness of visual generation by measuring reference-view divergence. IAPO rewards tool-use attention by measuring attribution alignment with a teacher. Both use visual attention as the process reward, but in opposite directions: PRPO's signal reflects where the model *shouldn't* look (divergence from reference), while IAPO's signal reflects where the model *should* look (convergence to teacher attribution). IAPO partially closes Gap #7 (visual faithfulness as VLM RL training signal) by showing that attention-based rewards can be defined for the discriminative (VQA + tool use) domain, not just the generative domain. Gap #7 remains open for the generative domain beyond PRPO's RVD.
