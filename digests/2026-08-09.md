**CSCR introduces a fifth orthogonal axis for OPSD token-level credit calibration.** The vault's teacher calibration taxonomy now has five axes: (A) ADRS-TVA — confidence-return correlation: does teacher confidence predict rollout success? (B) PCSD — temporal persistence: does the teacher maintain a consistent advantage over consecutive positions? (C) OCSD — scaffold isolation: does the teacher's score reflect observation content vs. replay scaffold? (D) DASH — sequential divergence history: does the local divergence fit the within-rollout discrepancy sequence? (E) CSCR — **counterfactual robustness**: does the teacher's direction change when the outcome condition is flipped? Where axes A–D ask *how much* weight to assign the teacher signal at various granularities, axis E asks whether the signal direction is reliable at all. CSCR's diagnosis (privileged shifts concentrate on surface-form tokens regardless of outcome condition) is a new empirical finding about OPSD that none of A–D address. Gap #19 expands to A×B×C×D×E; the combined five-axis calibration framework remains open.

**State2State introduces a fifth structural class in the agentic RL training paradigm taxonomy.** The existing four classes: EnvACE (policy-as-environment via world rehearsal — policy IS the environment), EnvFactory (automated real-world API environment synthesis — real environments at scale), DPEPO (explicit diversity rewards across simultaneous environments — diversity as first-class signal), AXPO (prefix-fixing resampling of all-wrong subgroups — credit-side fix). State2State's fifth class: **self-directed environment exploration as curriculum generator** — agents explore environments autonomously and derive their own training objectives from discovered states, verified via rule-based state matching. No external task specification, no expert trajectories, no handcrafted verifiers. The mid-training paradigm (environment-learning stage before downstream RL) is also new — none of the existing four classes position themselves as pre-RL initialization rather than end-to-end RL methods.

**Beyond Simply Environment Scaling provides the empirical counterpoint to EnvFactory's scale-first approach.** EnvFactory (Aug 8) built 85 environments from real-world APIs on the premise that more diverse environments → stronger tool-use agents. This paper shows directly that simply scaling environment count yields diminishing returns — diversity quality (AES: ability-aware selection) and difficulty structure (HDC: harness weakening + state-scale progression) are the real levers. This is the first direct empirical challenge to the environment-scaling paradigm within the vault's multimodal agent thread. The AES-HDC framework is complementary to EnvFactory: EnvFactory provides the methodology for constructing environments from real-world APIs; AES-HDC provides the methodology for selecting and structuring them once constructed.

**IBPO adds implicit multi-trajectory credit to the vault's credit granularity taxonomy.** The vault's credit taxonomy covers: token-level (ADRS, PCSD, OCSD, DASH, RP-OPSD, CSCR), step-level (Guided-OPD, FutureBridge-OPD), trajectory-level (TACO/DAPR counterfactual), and path-level (Parallel Shapley). IBPO sits between trajectory-level and step-level: it derives step-sensitive signals from within-batch trajectory differences without requiring an explicit process reward model or teacher. Unlike Parallel Shapley (cooperative game theory for path credit), IBPO uses trajectory divergence as an implicit counterfactual — no attribution model, no privileged teacher. This is a new mechanism that complements rather than replaces the existing taxonomy entries.
