**CompPO is the most structurally precise paper in today's digest.** The CCT framework's decomposition of credit transport into (evidence, transport operator, update geometry) directly names what GRPO's group-relative broadcast and fixed-discount GAE both get wrong: both use an architecture-agnostic transport operator. CompPO's attention-concentration retention gate is the first transport operator derived from the Transformer's own computation rather than from a generic discount schedule or a group statistic. The +7.6pp over GRPO (61.4% vs 53.8% on 5-seed Qwen3-4B) is the largest single-paper improvement seen in this digest series for GRPO-class methods.

**CANOPY directly challenges the "dense credit is necessary" consensus from Sep 11.** T1, TASPO, CRISP, and PLVR all add density to the credit signal in different ways; CANOPY argues the limiting factor is not credit density but exploration coverage and policy drift prevention. If CANOPY's 86.9% AppWorld score is reproducible without test-time interaction budget expansion, it would definitively falsify the dense-credit-is-necessary claim. If the test-time budget is load-bearing, CANOPY and the dense-credit papers are complementary along two different axes (training signal vs. inference compute). This is the most important empirical question from today's digest.

**CE-GRPO and CompPO together define representation-optimization co-design as a distinct research direction.** CE-GRPO makes representation the credit boundary (semantic units = optimization units). CompPO makes the model's internal computation the credit transport medium (attention = causal kernel). Both depart from GRPO's architecture-agnostic design in different directions: CE-GRPO targets the credit boundary, CompPO targets the credit transport. Together they suggest that GRPO's architecture-agnosticism, while making it broadly applicable, is leaving systematic gains on the table.

**LURE synthesizes two previously separate threads.** The learnability-calibrated curriculum thread (FrogNano, Sep 10 excluded) and the dense process credit thread (T1, TASPO, CRISP, Sep 11) merge in LURE's pursuit-evasion game: the evader's capture-frontier reward IS the learnability calibration, and the pursuer's capture-anchored process credit IS the dense signal. LURE is therefore not an additive combination of both threads but a game-theoretic unification: learnability calibration and credit density become joint outputs of the same optimization problem.

**TreeCredit resolves a credit assignment sub-gap within multi-agent reasoning that SRPO (Sep 10) left open.** SRPO addresses the optimization objective for multi-agent pipelines (active-set cardinality normalization) but assumes per-turn credit is given; TreeCredit addresses how that per-turn credit should be computed (state-matched downstream comparison via shared-prefix collaboration trees). The two papers together provide a more complete multi-agent RL recipe: SRPO for the inter-turn objective, TreeCredit for the per-turn credit.
