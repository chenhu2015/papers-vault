**Skill Entropy formally closes Gap #21 (complexity threshold for skill management).** Gap #21 has been tracked since Aug 13 with BCSD (indirect proxy via skill utilization supervision) as the closest approximation. Skill Entropy provides the formal definition: a pairwise measure of cross-skill switching difficulty over skill transition pairs, producing a task-level score. Skill-Entropy RL operationalizes the threshold as a training signal: the model is penalized when predicted skill sequences diverge from gold skill sequences, directly rewarding skill-switching competence proportional to its entropy. The BCSD proxy (Aug 14) is superseded — Gap #21 now has a formal answer at both the measurement (Skill²-Bench) and optimization (Skill-Entropy RL) levels.

**The Horizon Gap survey provides a second independent validation of the vault's step-level signal framing.** The CA survey (Aug 13) validated the vault's credit assignment taxonomy at 69 papers. The Horizon Gap validates it at 1,547 papers across a broader scope (not just CA, but training/evaluation/planning/memory). The field-wide convergence finding — that outcome-only signals grow uninformative as horizons lengthen, and the universal response is denser step-level signals — is the empirical basis for every paper in the vault's process reward, OPD, and GRPO variant threads. The open correlated-bias problem (process signals used for both training and evaluation) directly maps to Gap #19's calibration problem applied to the evaluation axis.

**PASS@(k,T) separates two questions the vault has been conflating.** The vault's credit assignment, OPD, and GRPO variant papers all assume RL training improves agent performance, but PASS@(k,T) distinguishes *capability expansion* (RL unlocks strategies the base model cannot execute at any k) from *reliability improvement* (RL makes existing strategies more consistent). For compositional tool-use, RL expands capability; for simpler tasks, it only improves reliability. This means the vault's training-mechanism papers (PAIR, CSO, RSPO, SRGPO, Temporal GRPO, etc.) should be evaluated against a capability-expansion standard on compositional tasks, not just aggregate accuracy improvement.

**SeekJudge adds a fourth trajectory-verdict architecture to the reward model taxonomy.** The vault's reward/verifier mechanisms for agentic RL: PAIR (internal LLM hidden-state probe, Aug 15), VERDICT (disagreement-consensus across verifiers, Aug 14), VPS/VPRM (rule-based step verifiers, earlier). SeekJudge adds role-specialized multi-agent verdict via a Seek-Analyze loop on a shared distilled 9B backbone. The four-role decomposition (Condense → Ground → Seek → Analyze) is architecturally distinct from VERDICT's consensus (same judgment function, multiple instances) and from PAIR's internalist approach (no external agent call). SeekJudge occupies the "external, task-decomposed, distilled" quadrant of the verdict architecture space.

**ToolRL-DR introduces POMDP-component robustness as a new axis for tool-use agent evaluation.** The vault has tracked what RL training methods optimize for (credit, process reward, supervision source). ToolRL-DR introduces the dual question: what deployment perturbations break the trained agent, and by POMDP component? The finding that transition perturbations are scale-invariant (~30% drop regardless of model size, 1.5B–32B) but partially closed by domain-randomized RL on *non-transition* perturbations connects to SOD's cascading tool-call error amplification (Aug 14 confirmed novel): ToolRL-DR's retry-policy transfer may be the mechanism by which RL on adversarial static inputs partially mitigates cascading dynamic failures.
