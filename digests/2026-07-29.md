CIGPO (0.87) is the most structurally significant paper today and the most direct partial closure of Gap #16 found since Dark Room (Jul 25) opened it. Dark Room diagnosed GRPO's all-fail group pathology mechanistically; CIGPO provides a concrete solution from the per-turn reward design angle: inject information-gain rewards that maintain within-group variance even when all trajectory-level outcomes are identical. The CIGPO IG signal (marginal increase in reference log-likelihood per turn) is a per-turn instantiation of Progress Advantage's log π_RL/π_ref (Jul 26): Progress Advantage measures the final log-ratio as the optimal step-level advantage; CIGPO's IG is the turn-by-turn increment of the same quantity, applied before GRPO normalization rather than after. This means CIGPO's per-turn signal is estimating the marginal contribution of each evidence-reading turn to the total progress advantage — a decomposition that Progress Advantage did not make but CIGPO implicitly implements for multi-turn retrieval tasks.

**New connection — CIGPO as turn-level decomposition of Progress Advantage:** Progress Advantage (Jul 26) proved that log π_RL/π_ref is the optimal step-level advantage over the full trajectory. CIGPO's per-turn IG = Δ log P_ref(y*|context_t) is the marginal increase in the reference model's confidence in the ground truth after turn t's evidence is added to the context. Under a consistent reference model, the sum of per-turn IGs over the full trajectory approximates the total progress advantage — making CIGPO's reward a turn-level decomposition of the quantity Progress Advantage proved to be optimal. This connection was not noted by the CIGPO paper and suggests a theoretical bridge: if Progress Advantage is optimal for the full trajectory, then CIGPO's per-turn IG decomposition is the natural way to distribute that optimal reward across turns, which explains why it succeeds where vanilla GRPO fails.

OC-GRPO (0.80) addresses the extreme case of Gap #16: zero-signal cliff where no correct solutions exist (group reward = 0 for all members → group relative advantage = 0 for all members → no gradient). Dark Room addresses the intermediate case (bounded dense rewards in all-fail groups amplify pathologically under z-normalization); CIGPO addresses the multi-turn format-penalty case (all members receive minimum format penalty → advantage collapses); OC-GRPO addresses the extreme case (no correct solution → zero reward everywhere). The IS-correction mechanism in OC-GRPO is the same mathematical tool as SIS (below) but applied at the rollout level rather than the token level.

SIS (0.77) and ARMOR (Jul 28, 0.75) are now the two canonical approaches to off-policy data integration in on-policy LLM RL: ARMOR (add anchor reference rollouts to the batch; mix optimization objective) vs. SIS (selectively accept/reject off-policy tokens via rejection sampling). The key difference: ARMOR's anchor rollouts are a data augmentation strategy that changes what trajectories are included; SIS's rejection sampling is a gradient correction strategy that changes how existing off-policy tokens are weighted. They are composable — SIS could be applied on top of ARMOR's mixed batch.

ReSkill (0.75) introduces the most novel use of GRPO's group structure seen in the vault to date: rather than using within-group variance to estimate token-level or turn-level credit (as in CIGPO, P-GRPO, STAPO), ReSkill uses within-group rollout diversity to compare discrete versioned skills under matched policy conditions. The Thompson Sampling + adaptive discounting mechanism is the key innovation: it treats skill selection as a multi-armed bandit problem that must account for the non-stationarity of the policy (older skill comparisons become irrelevant as the policy distributes shift). The automatic skill lifecycle (create → test → refine → prune) is the agentic RL analogue of neural architecture search: skills are the architecture, GRPO group structure is the evaluation harness, Thompson Sampling is the search algorithm.
