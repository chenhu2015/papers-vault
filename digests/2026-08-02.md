**TACO introduces the first judge-free per-tool-call credit mechanism in the vault.** All prior tool-use credit papers (TAO-RL, GRSD, RLVMR) either reward post-tool-call tokens statistically (TAO-RL), use NL reflection within groups (GRSD), or annotate meta-reasoning behavior (RLVMR). TACO's DAPR uses the answer checker itself as the credit signal by probing the model's predictions with and without the tool — the credit is a difference in outcome probability, not a separate model's assessment. This makes it the first self-supervised tool-call credit mechanism that is: (a) per-tool-call rather than per-token, (b) sign-aware (positive/negative/neutral), and (c) requires no external judge. OGAR's parameter-free routing further prevents wasted-tool credit diffusion without any explicit penalty term, complementing DAPR's per-call valuation with segment-level routing.

**UnifiedReward-Think closes Gap #4 (dedicated VLM reward model training).** The gap has been open since July 22 with multiple failed searches. UnifiedReward-Think is the first paper in the vault that trains a reward model with explicit internal reasoning for multimodal tasks, using GRPO to explore diverse correct reasoning paths. Its three-stage pipeline (distillation cold start → rejection sampling → GRPO on incorrect samples) is structurally analogous to how the Gap #16 papers (ProGPO, GRSD) treat incorrect-outcome groups: rather than discarding or penalizing them, they extract training signal from failures. The "implicit reasoning improves direct-response accuracy" finding is also notable — it suggests that even when deployed as a scalar reward model, the internal CoT training provides a regularization benefit.

**SGCD defines a third NL-contrast credit source.** GRSD (Jul 31) used the policy's own reflection to contrast success vs. failure within a group. SGCD uses an external LLM to contrast sibling rollouts across a task. Together with TACO's self-probing, there are now three distinct NL-based credit sources: self-reflection (GRSD), self-probing (TACO), and external LLM contrast (SGCD). The "keep policy gradient in charge" design principle in SGCD — using distillation only for bounded credit weighting, not as a competing actor loss — generalizes to all three: GRSD uses stop-gradient snapshots, TACO uses detached probe tokens, SGCD uses detached divergence reshaping.

**UCOB integrates skill lifecycle management with bidirectional credit.** The vault's skill lifecycle synthesis (SkillRise + Bayesian-Agent + ReSkill) was previously characterized as three separate components: skill evolution (SkillRise), lifecycle state management (Bayesian-Agent), and version selection (ReSkill). UCOB's return-to-go comparison integrates all three functions: the credit signal determines when a skill-conditioned view is trustworthy (→ retrieval gate), which behaviors to internalize (→ evolution), and how to update the skill memory (→ selection). UCOB does not separate lifecycle state management from credit assignment — it derives both from the same return-to-go comparison at anchor states.

**GOPO diagnoses a new GRPO failure mode: absolute vs. ordinal misalignment.** The prior Gap #16 family addressed the zero-magnitude problem (all-fail groups where all rewards equal zero). GOPO addresses a different problem: when reward models assign non-zero magnitudes that are internally ordinal but externally absolute, GRPO's advantage computation imports irrelevant scale. This is most acute in preference-based reward settings. GOPO and ProGPO are now the two "reward transformation" papers in the vault — ProGPO transforms zero-outcome rewards to state-novelty proxies, GOPO transforms all rewards to ordinal ranks. Their combination (ordinal-transform non-fail groups + proxy-substitute all-fail groups) would be a complete treatment of GRPO reward distortion, and is a natural Gap #17 extension.
