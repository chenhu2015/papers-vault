**StructReward closes the reward-signal gap in GRPO that MaxPO and OTB left open.** MaxPO (Aug 31) proved GRPO advantages are non-centered and proposed L2O centering. OTB (Sep 01) proved group baselines ignore within-sequence token heterogeneity and proposed per-token gradient reweighting. Both work on the advantage estimator. StructReward works on the reward signal itself: replacing binary outcome reward with dense structured process reward inside the GRPO objective. The three papers constitute a complete GRPO improvement stack: (1) center the advantage (MaxPO L2O), (2) weight per-token gradients (OTB), (3) densify the reward signal (StructReward). No paper studies all three together.

**SPEAR provides a concrete mechanism for OPD origin alignment.** OPD Generalization (Sep 01) proved that teacher-student origin alignment determines transfer quality but did not specify what makes origins aligned or misaligned. SPEAR's symbolic milestone projection gives a proxy answer: origins are aligned when the teacher's symbolic milestone vocabulary maps to the student's domain. When a teacher trained on math uses math-specific symbolic milestones (equations, variable assignments, proof steps), LCS alignment is meaningful only for student domains where those milestones appear. Cross-domain (e.g., math teacher → commonsense student) produces milestones that the student rarely traverses, degrading LCS reward — which is exactly the origin-misalignment degradation OPD Generalization observed empirically. SPEAR resolves this via domain-adaptive milestone sets.

**HARTS + TeamTR form a complementary systems+theory pair for multi-agent agentic RL.** TeamTR (Aug 31) formalized the policy-stale data problem (O(N²) compounding occupancy shift) in multi-LLM sequential fine-tuning. HARTS formalizes and solves the compute-redundancy problem in individual agent rollout trees (O(prefix_length) redundant recomputation per branch). Both problems scale with N (agents) and tree depth; TeamTR's trust-region resampling controls the policy-staleness axis while HARTS's prefix compression controls the compute-efficiency axis. Together they may make large-scale multi-agent agentic RL (e.g., SAT-style systems at N=10+) tractable.

**WM-R1 extends world-model RL to GUI agents, connecting to VAGEN via a different axis.** VAGEN (Aug 22) used world models to reason about multi-turn VLM agent state in non-GUI environments. WM-R1 uses world models as full environment replacements for GUI RL training, embedding the world model in the thinking chain. The distinction: VAGEN uses the world model for state-representation; WM-R1 uses it for environment simulation. This opens a new design axis in agentic RL: use world models for planning (WM-R1) vs. use real environment with efficient rollout sharing (HARTS). The two approaches are complementary — HARTS's prefix sharing could accelerate WM-R1's rollout generation.

**REDAgentBench triangulates the faithfulness failure space from the opposite direction of FACE-Eval.** FACE-Eval (Sep 01) identified covert adoption: agents act on tool-return information without verbalizing it (CoT blind to what drives action). REDAgentBench identifies overt non-compliance: agents verbalize a constraint but still violate it (action blind to what CoT states). The bidirectional failure — CoT ≠ action in both directions — suggests a deeper disconnect between reasoning trace and execution trace that neither SRPO (Sep 01) nor FACE-Eval addresses at the training level. The policy reminder intervention (70+ pp reduction) shows this gap can be patched inference-time, but the root cause in RL training remains open.
