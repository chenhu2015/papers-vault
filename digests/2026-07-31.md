**New synthesis — entropy-as-structural-signal family now has five distinct instances:** STAPO (Jul 26, normalized entropy for neglect-prone steps) → O²-CritiCuRL (Jul 28, multi-rollout frequency for critical steps) → TAO-RL (Jul 30, entropy bonus at post-tool-call tokens) → ERPO (today, CDPs as high-entropy trajectory forks) → STARE (today, surprisal quantiles for entropy-critical tokens) → ICT (today, JS divergence for distributional deviation). The family now spans: (1) offline multi-rollout analysis (O²-CritiCuRL), (2) online entropy gating (ERPO/STAPO), (3) online surprisal-based selection (STARE), (4) distributional deviation measurement (ICT), (5) post-decision-point entropy bonus (TAO-RL). These are five distinct *implementations* of the same principle: weight the credit signal by how informative a token/step's uncertainty is.

**STARE provides the first theoretical unification of the family.** Its advantage-surprisal decomposition — per-token entropy variation = trajectory advantage × entropy sensitivity function — proves that entropy-critical tokens are exactly those where the advantage signal most amplifies entropy change. This means any entropy-based token selector (STAPO's normalized entropy, ERPO's CDPs, ICT's JS divergence, TAO-RL's post-tool entropy) is implicitly approximating the same entropy sensitivity function. STARE's result makes Gap #12 (entropy as structural signal) substantially closed for the token-level case: the theoretical principle is now established, and the five instances are all approximations of the same gradient-level quantity.

**ERPO's three-component synthesis is the most compositionally complete token-level GRPO variant seen:** entropy gating (CDPs) + difficulty normalization (bucket-based) + outcome anchoring (result-anchored advantage) combines approaches that appear separately in STAPO (entropy gating), RDPO (normalization), and GRAIL (gradient-based reweighting). No prior paper combines all three components. ERPO's date (March 2026) places it before STARE (June 2026), making it a precursor that STARE's theoretical analysis retroactively explains.

**SkillRise introduces cross-episode credit assignment** — a new credit granularity not previously in the vault. While all prior credit assignment papers operate within an episode (token, turn, step, branch), SkillRise's skill curation reward flows from downstream task outcomes, making the credit signal cross-episode. This is the natural extension of the credit assignment hierarchy: token → turn → step → branch → episode → cross-episode. SkillRise occupies the cross-episode level. The decoupled credit (current-task for solving, discounted downstream for curation) prevents the credit from propagating too noisily across the episode boundary, analogous to how CIGPO's per-turn IG prevents the full-trajectory reward from being applied uniformly.

**JitRL completes a new axis: training-loop RL ↔ inference-time RL.** All prior vault papers operate inside the training loop (policy gradient updates, GRPO, reward model training). JitRL proves that the KL-constrained policy optimization objective has a closed-form solution at test time via additive logit modulation — no gradients needed. The KL log π_RL/π_ref term in JitRL's objective is the same term that appears in Progress Advantage (optimal advantage = log π_RL/π_ref) and CIGPO's IG signal (marginal increase in reference log-likelihood). JitRL establishes that this term is tractable at inference time via non-parametric memory retrieval, making it the first paper to "solve" the KL objective in the inference-time regime rather than the training-loop regime.

**Updated synthesis — entropy-as-structural-signal (Gap #12 now substantially closed):** With STARE's gradient analysis proving the advantage-surprisal decomposition, Gap #12 transitions from "three instances suggesting a pattern" to "five instances with theoretical grounding."
