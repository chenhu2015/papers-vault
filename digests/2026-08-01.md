**A²TGPO closes the IG normalization open problems.** When the IGPO→InfoReasoner→InfoPO→CIGPO lineage was characterized (Jul 28–30), three systematic challenges were identified as open: positional normalization distortion, magnitude drift with trajectory depth, and fixed clipping regardless of IG content. A²TGPO (May 2026) addresses all three simultaneously, making it a retroactive completion of the lineage. Its turn-group normalization (compare only within same turn-index) is structurally analogous to CIGPO's reference log-likelihood IG (compare only within same trajectory position), and its adaptive clipping is the first paper to directly modulate the PPO clip range using the IG signal itself. The combination of variance-rescaled accumulation + adaptive clipping makes A²TGPO the most complete IG-based turn-level RL paper seen so far.

**ProGPO introduces the 7th distinct Gap #16 fix and defines a new solution class.** The six prior approaches all either modify what happens within groups (reward shaping, variance injection, IS correction) or filter/remove degenerate groups (TAO-RL). ProGPO introduces a third structural class: *conditional proxy reward substitution* — replacing the zero outcome reward within all-failed groups with a state-novelty proxy, conditioned on the group-level outcome distribution. This is complementary to TAO-RL (which removes all-failed groups) and to CIGPO (which injects IG variance into the advantage). The ProGPO+TAO-RL combination would: remove all-failed groups where state novelty proxy is uninformative (all trajectories revisit the same states), and activate state novelty where there is genuine coverage variance across trajectories. No paper yet proposes this combination.

**GRSD introduces a new process supervision source: within-group contrastive reflection.** Prior process supervision approaches use statistics (IG, entropy, surprisal) or external annotations. GRSD generates process supervision from the policy's own language-model reflections by contrasting what it says about successful vs. failed trajectories in the same group. This is a capability-adaptive approach: the quality of the guidance scales with the policy's reflective ability, which grows during training. The stop-gradient snapshot mechanism prevents the reflection quality from destabilizing training — an important engineering choice not discussed in prior self-distillation papers. GRSD occupies a unique position in the process-supervision taxonomy: it derives turn-level credit from group-level natural language reasoning rather than group-level numerical statistics.

**RLVMR provides the first domain-knowledge-based process supervision approach in the vault.** All prior process supervision papers (IG family, entropy family, GRSD) derive process rewards from trajectory statistics. RLVMR instead specifies cognitive behaviors (plan/explore/reflect tagging) and rewards them verifiably. This makes the process reward interpretable and human-auditable but requires domain knowledge about what constitutes good meta-reasoning. The 83.6% result on ALFWorld's hardest split with a 7B model is the highest reported for that benchmark in the vault, suggesting meta-reasoning supervision provides an orthogonal signal to the statistical approaches.
