**OCSD introduces a third orthogonal axis for agentic OPD calibration.** The vault's teacher calibration taxonomy now has three axes: (A) ADRS-TVA — confidence-return correlation: does the teacher's confidence at this token predict rollout success within the current group? (B) PCSD — temporal persistence: does the teacher maintain a consistent advantage over consecutive positions? (C) OCSD — scaffold isolation: does the teacher's score change reflect the observation's content or the replay scaffold's structural effect? These axes are independent — ADRS-TVA operates at the group-outcome level, PCSD at the token-sequence level, OCSD at the replay-mechanism level. A combined A×B×C calibration gate would form a complete multi-axis teacher signal for agentic OPD; no paper yet combines all three. This closes the active search thread from Gap #19 with a concrete third axis.

**Dark Room provides the structural account of why all-fail z-scoring is the core failure mechanism for dense rewards in GRPO.** The vault's Gap #16 literature has characterized 10 approaches to all-fail group filtering, but none had formally explained *why* all-fail groups are dangerous for dense reward signals (as opposed to sparse outcome rewards). Dark Room's key result is that in all-fail groups, z-score normalization cancels the shaping coefficient entirely — making the dense signal not merely noisy but actively counterproductive. This retrodict explains why CIGPO, TAO-RL, and AXPO's group-level interventions work: they prevent the z-score cancellation by ensuring the group has variance. It also explains OCSD's high-uncertainty modulation: OCSD applies the calibrated residual specifically at high-uncertainty steps, which are exactly the steps where within-group variance is non-trivial and the z-score cancellation is least likely.

**Parallel Shapley extends credit assignment from the token level to the path level using cooperative game theory.** The vault's credit assignment taxonomy covers token-level (ADRS, PCSD, OCSD), step-level (Guided-OPD, FutureBridge-OPD), and trajectory-level (TACO/DAPR counterfactual). Parallel Shapley introduces a fourth granularity: path-level credit in parallel multi-path reasoning. The free-rider concept (paths that receive uniform reward despite contributing little) is structurally isomorphic to TACO's DAPR (counterfactual "what-if-no-tool" probing), but Parallel Shapley is domain-agnostic (applies to any parallel generation, not just tool calls) and cooperative-game-theoretic (Shapley marginal contribution vs. counterfactual differential).

**RL Post-Training Builds Compositional Reasoning provides mechanistic grounding for the skill lifecycle synthesis.** The vault's skill lifecycle papers (SkillRise, Bayesian-Agent, ReSkill, UCOB, SPyCE, Skill-α) treat explicit skill engineering as necessary for compositional generalization. This paper shows vanilla RL post-training discovers sequential and parallel compositional strategies in a controlled environment without any skill engineering. This does not invalidate the skill lifecycle synthesis — the grammar environment is simpler than agentic multi-tool tasks — but it raises the question: for which task complexity level does explicit skill management become necessary? This is a natural open question for the vault's skill thread.

**Switch-Reasoner connects multimodal GRPO to the adaptive-curriculum thread.** The dual-level regulation mechanism (global mode balance + sample-level relative benefit) is structurally similar to the SFT Conflicts/RL Coexists Parallel-RL decoupling: both separate global training balance from local update decisions. Switch-Reasoner applies this to mode selection (think vs. direct) rather than task separation, but the dual-level structure is the same. In the AXPO/SPyCE multimodal RL thread (Aug 3), Switch-Reasoner fills a gap: where AXPO addresses sample efficiency (all-wrong tool-using subgroups) and SPyCE addresses knowledge reuse (skill co-evolution), Switch-Reasoner addresses compute efficiency (unnecessary reasoning reduction).
