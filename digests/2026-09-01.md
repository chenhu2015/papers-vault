**OTB closes the per-token variance gap that MaxPO left open.** MaxPO (Aug 31) proved GRPO's group-based advantages are non-centered (non-zero batch sum) and proposed L2O centering as a fix. OTB proves that group-based baselines — even after L2O centering — overlook within-sequence token heterogeneity: each token's gradient contribution should be weighted inversely by its cumulative gradient norm, not uniformly. The Logit-Gradient Proxy makes this computable from forward-pass probabilities only. The combined MaxPO + OTB prescription: (1) apply L2O centering to eliminate batch-sum bias, then (2) apply OTB per-token reweighting to eliminate within-sequence variance. Neither paper cites the other — complementary independent discoveries, empirical combination gap open.

**OPD Generalization resolves the origin-relationship question that AToD and OPDVR left implicit.** Both AToD (Aug 29) and OPDVR (Aug 30) assume the teacher's knowledge is transferable to the student domain; neither asks when and how much transfers. This paper's answer — transfer depends on teacher-student origin alignment, not training difficulty or dataset size — reframes the AToD annealing design: the "transition timing" variable (when to shift from distillation to RL) matters only if the teacher origin aligns with the student domain. For cross-origin teacher-student pairs, even heavy distillation may mostly fit the training distribution without cross-domain transfer, making the RL phase more important throughout. The multi-teacher seesaw also directly constrains TeamTR (Aug 31): the O(N²) compounding occupancy shift that TeamTR's trust-region approach controls may interact with teacher-origin seesaw effects when the N agents come from different domain origins.

**FACE-Eval provides empirical grounding for the SRPO faithfulness gap from a different angle.** SRPO faithfulness (open since Aug 27) asks whether self-generated reflection patches identify causal vs. post-hoc-rationalized steps. FACE-Eval asks whether CoT traces faithfully record which information drives the model's answer when that information arrives via tool returns. These are distinct questions, but FACE-Eval's finding is load-bearing for the agentic RL thread: agents trained with RL to use tools (ZeroTIR Aug 29, GBT Aug 31, EDGE Aug 22) become better at acting on tool-return information — and FACE-Eval suggests this capability improvement comes with a faithfulness cost. The tool-return vs. user-message faithfulness gap may widen as tool-use RL training improves.
