**T1 provides the first systematic treatment of training-inference drift in long-horizon MoE RL.** TITO (token-identifier alignment with turn-boundary drift repair) and rollout routing replay (per-token expert choice recording) address two previously unmeasured drift sources that accumulate over 300+ turn trajectories. The dense verifier-counting reward is the concrete realization of the Long-Horizon PPM thread (Sep 10 excluded paper): per-turn absolute passing-verifier count rather than binary terminal success. T1 + Long-Horizon PPM together close the "dense credit for very long chains" sub-gap from two angles: T1 from a deployed system perspective, PPM from an algorithmic perspective.

**TASPO formally resolves the supervision-credit gap left open by ExecCritic and ERPO (Sep 09).** Both ExecCritic and ERPO use execution artifacts as reward but do not address how process-level signals should be weighted relative to verified terminal outcomes. TASPO's mean-preserving weight redistribution is the correct formal answer: outcome determines gradient direction and average scale, while PI (privileged information from verified successes) only redistributes within-trajectory. This is structurally analogous to GRPO's group normalization within a prompt — an intra-trajectory normalization that preserves the outcome-determined inter-trajectory gradient.

**CRISP's Backward Evidence Induction and TASPO's PI construction are the same mechanism from different angles.** Both use hindsight information from completed trajectories (CRISP: backward labeling from final answer; TASPO: PI from verified successful experience) to assign more accurate per-step credit. The difference is in how that credit is applied: CRISP uses step-level labels to shape the training reward (evidence-critical steps preserved, redundant steps penalized), while TASPO uses PI-induced likelihood shifts as mean-preserving weights on trajectory advantage. Together they define a general pattern: hindsight-informed credit redistribution, which is the common thread linking TRIAL (signed log-probability gap), T-STAR (cognitive tree back-propagation), TASPO (mean-preserving PI weighting), and CRISP (backward evidence induction).

**Ecdysis operationalizes the model-harness gap (Sep 08) at the diagnostic level.** The model-specific vs. harness-level failure distinction is the precise operationalization of "compatibility as an explicit objective" identified from Co-Evolving Harnesses. Ecdysis provides the diagnosis layer (cross-instance failure aggregation → systematic vs. model-specific distinction) that would be necessary for a joint model-harness optimization system. The gap (no paper trains model weights and harness jointly with compatibility as an explicit objective) remains open, but Ecdysis reduces it from a conceptual gap to a missing optimization objective.

**PLVR is structurally the most distinct paper in this digest.** Rather than assigning denser rewards to the same trajectory (T1, TASPO, CRISP), PLVR proposes a different substrate: reasoning placed outside model weights as explicitly typed programs, with credit assignment as type-inference derivation. The per-step contract verdict is dense because program structure itself is the verification unit. This is the symbolic analog of ERPO's probe consensus — both use structural properties (type contracts vs. behavioral agreement) rather than learned judges. PLVR's primitive library transferability (one library, two benchmarks, no new fine-tuning) is also the strongest argument yet for the programs-as-reasoning alternative to RLVR.
