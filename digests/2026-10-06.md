GACA introduces the first analytically-derived mixing coefficient for granularity selection — prior papers chose a fixed granularity (SHARPO: segment, TPAE: token, FAULT: segment) while GACA adapts per-decision. AdaStep (shrinkage for reliability) and GACA (NLL-based mixing for granularity) are now two orthogonal meta-layers that could compose: use AdaStep to decide whether step-level credit is reliable, then GACA to decide how much episode-level credit to blend in.

**BoostAPR learned vs. DepGPO graph-derived execution credit**: BoostAPR trains a line-level credit allocator from execution outcomes; DepGPO uses automatically-derived command dependency graphs. The open question is whether a learned allocator (BoostAPR style) can capture the same causal structure as an automatically-derived dependency graph (DepGPO style) without requiring execution-verified training data. A comparison on a shared benchmark (SWE-Gym) would clarify this.
